# The target: what a finished 7EIU assessment looks like

Every skill in this team writes against one reference document, the assessment in
`reference_assessment.md`. That is the target. If a section you write would not sit
comfortably beside it, the section is wrong.

The reference is a complete, invented engagement. Read it before you write anything. This
file describes its shape; that file is the thing itself.

---

## 1. What the document actually is

An outside read of one company, organized by the seven laws, written by one person to one
person. It is not a report. It is a letter with headings.

Eight to ten pages. Roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words of prose plus one or two full-page
diagrams. Every observation in it came out of an interview and whatever the owner handed
over. Nothing in it was researched from the outside and dressed up as insight.

The tone: someone who spent time inside the business telling the owner what they saw,
including the parts they did not get to see.

---

## 2. Page-by-page anatomy

**Cover.** Running header with the author's name and city on the left, email and phone on
the right. Brand mark centered beneath. A small-caps kicker (`POST MORTEM`, `DIAGNOSTIC`,
`ASSESSMENT`). The title is the client name plus the engagement. A one-line subtitle in
grey: `An evaluation using the 7 EIU principles`. At the foot, two columns: `PREPARED FOR`
(name, title, company, address) and `PREPARED BY` (name, company, city, email, phone).

**The framing paragraph.** Three to five sentences. It states the vantage point, says which
laws the company already satisfies, and names where the leverage is. It does not open with
a summary of findings. It tells the reader where the assessor is standing, gives a verdict
fast, and points at one place. Where the assessor's first read turned out wrong, saying so
is better than hiding it.

**The seven-principles diagram.** A full-width figure directly under the framing paragraph,
captioned `7 Principles Diagram`. All seven laws in their three groups, focus law
highlighted. See `diagram_seven_principles.html`.

**The differentiator paragraph.** One paragraph on what the company has that is not the
product. It comes from the "what do you have that is not the product" question in the
intake.

**A one-line hand-off.** Something like: *Assuming you already know that, the next step is
to run the second crew through the seven principles.* Then the sections start.

**Seven principle sections.** `PRINCIPLE 1: Intent & Action` through
`PRINCIPLE 7: Karma & Value`, each a small bold blue heading, each followed by one to five
short paragraphs. Length varies with how much the assessor actually learned. **Do not pad
the thin ones to match the thick ones.** The unevenness is honest and it is part of the
voice.

**Opportunity diagrams.** Where one law carries the biggest opportunity, a full-page figure
follows its section. Usually `THE MARKETING GARDEN` under Principle 4: a header card naming
the asset that already exists, the law stated in the client's terms, then six labelled
cards, each with a category word, a name, and one line of what it is for. At most two
opportunity figures per document.

**Principle 7 as the synthesis.** The last section introduces no new material. It opens
`The previous 6 principles suggest the following:` and walks the six earlier
recommendations in law order in a single paragraph, closing on a forward-looking statement
of belief about the client's own stated goal, in the client's own words.

---

## 3. How a section is built

Four moves. Not all four appear every time, and not in a fixed order.

1. **The observation.** Something the assessor actually saw or was told, stated plainly and
   attributed to where it came from. *"You said that without a run-up."* *"You told me you
   assumed price."*

2. **The law applied.** The observation read through the law, in the law's own terms but
   without lecturing. The law is never quoted at the reader; it is used.

3. **The opportunity.** What to do, at the size the company actually is, specific enough to
   start on. Where the move is blocked until another law's finding shifts, say so and name
   the smaller thing that is startable now.

4. **The honest gap.** Where the assessor did not learn enough, they say so and ask, in
   italics. This move is what makes the rest credible.

## 4. Voice rules the reference obeys

Hard constraints. The `7eiu-mo-voice` and `7eiu-human-voice` skills carry the full rules;
these are the ones this document type turns on.

**First person and second person, throughout.** "I", "you". Never "the client", "the
organization", "stakeholders". The company's own name is fine and normal.

**Named people, first names, in the body.** An advisory letter, not a contract, so the
roles-over-names convention does not apply. Use the names the client used, and attach to
each an observation the owner would recognize as true and could show that person.

**Claims hedged to what was actually learned.** "it seems", "I suspect", "from my vantage
point", "there may be an opportunity", "I believe". The confidence lives in the specifics,
not in the verbs.

**Nothing invented, ever.** No statistics, no anecdotes, no clients, no staff, no "in my
experience", no track record. Any outside source is real, findable, and used for one narrow
point. If there is no evidence, the claim gets cut or turned into a question.

**No claimed expertise in the reader's trade.** The reader is the expert on their own
industry. Saying "I do not know your trade well enough to say" costs nothing and buys the
rest of the document.

**No AI tool names or version numbers.** Anywhere. The document says "AI tools" and what a
capability does. Same rule as the book, and it is what keeps the deliverable from expiring.

**Never name the older tradition behind the seven laws.** They are presented as Mo's
framework.

**Short paragraphs.** Three to six sentences. One-sentence paragraphs are fine.

**Italic bullets for questions and options.** Short, one line each. They carry questions
back to the client or a menu of assets. They never carry the argument. A menu may end in
*Etc.*

**Endings are forward-looking, not quotable.** Sections end on their content, on a question,
or on what happens next. No manufactured closing line.

**Client vocabulary, verbatim.** If they say "bottleneck", the document says bottleneck.

## 5. The tiered deliverables

Two documents from one intake.

**The Quick Read (2 pages).** Free, ungated, meant to be given away. Cover block, a
three-sentence framing paragraph, the seven-principles diagram, and seven blocks of two to
three sentences: one observation, one opportunity, per law. Closes by naming the single
highest-leverage law and what a full assessment would go after.

**The Full Assessment (8 to 10 pages).** The reference. Everything in section 2 above.

Both come from the same intake and the same chapter-skill output. The Quick Read is not a
summary written after the full one. It is the same findings at a different resolution, and
it is produced first, because writing it forces the ranking the full document needs.

---

## 6. Hard fails

A draft carrying any of these does not ship, regardless of its score.

- An AI product name or version number.
- An invented person, client, number, statistic, source, or anecdote.
- A claim about the reader's operations the intake does not support.
- The older tradition behind the laws, or any of its principle names, mentioned.
- A law applied to the wrong domain.
- A section ending on a manufactured quotable takeaway.
- Any of the seven sections missing, or present as a heading with nothing assessed under it.
- A Principle 7 synthesis that introduces new material or skips one of the six.

---

## 7. A note on confidentiality

An assessment names a real company, its staff, its customers and its weaknesses. It is a
private document between the assessor and the owner.

Do not reuse a real engagement as a teaching example, in this repository or anywhere else.
The reference in `reference_assessment.md` is invented for that reason. When you need a new
worked example, invent one and label it plainly as invented.
