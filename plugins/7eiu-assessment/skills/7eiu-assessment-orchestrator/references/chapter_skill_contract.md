# The chapter-skill contract

Seven skills in this team, one per law. They all honor the same interface so the
orchestrator can call them in any order and assemble the results without special cases.
If you add an eighth law to a future edition, build it to this contract.

## The map

| # | Skill | Law | Domain on the diagram | Section |
|---|-------|-----|----------------------|---------|
| 1 | `7eiu-law-1-intention-and-action` | Intention and Action | Leadership | Manifesting Thought |
| 2 | `7eiu-law-2-big-and-small` | Big and Small | Process | Manifesting Thought |
| 3 | `7eiu-law-3-being-consistent` | Being Consistent | Operations | Manifesting Thought |
| 4 | `7eiu-law-4-active-and-passive-marketing` | Active and Passive Marketing | Marketing | Building Relationships |
| 5 | `7eiu-law-5-wins-and-losses` | Wins and Losses | Support | Building Relationships |
| 6 | `7eiu-law-6-rhythm-and-fit` | Rhythm and Fit | Relations | Building Relationships |
| 7 | `7eiu-law-7-karma-and-value` | Karma and Value | Evaluation | Reflection |

The domain column is the boundary. A finding about how the company reaches strangers
belongs to Law 4 even if it also touches consistency. When two laws both want a finding,
the domain decides, and the other law may reference it in one clause without re-arguing it.

## What the orchestrator hands each chapter skill

- The intake record, with every entry marked `[said]`, `[doc]`, `[observed]` or `[gap]`.
- The company's twelve-month goal in the owner's own words.
- Any names, and what each person is good at.
- Which laws the framing paragraph already declared satisfied, so the skill can be brief
  where brevity is honest.

## What each chapter skill hands back

A block with five parts, returned in this shape so the orchestrator gets the same thing
from all seven:

```
verdict:       strong | working | thin | not observed
section:       |
  <prose>
quick_read:    <two or three sentences>
opportunities:
  - <move>
gaps:
  - <what could not be assessed, and the question to hand back>
blocked_on:    <optional: the law whose finding has to move first, and why>
diagram:       <optional proposal, or omit>
```

**Law 4 returns two verdicts**, because the law has two sides and a single word hides which
one is broken. Write it as `thin (passive) / working (active)`. The orchestrator ranks on
the weaker of the two.

**`blocked_on` is not an excuse, it is sequencing.** A recommendation can be right and still
be impossible until something else moves. A nine-person firm whose owner is the bottleneck
on every deliverable cannot build six marketing assets this season, however correct the
marketing finding is. Say so, name the law that has to move first, and keep the
recommendation. The orchestrator uses this to order Principle 7's walk. Without it the
document asks the most time-starved person in the company to start a project that assumes
spare capacity, and the owner reads it, agrees with it, and does nothing.

**Gaps in the intake record belong to whichever law's domain they sit in.** A `[gap]` about
who decides in the field is Law 1's. A `[gap]` about scheduling is Law 3's. A `[gap]` about
pricing or margins is Law 7's, because that law asks where the money comes from and what
the customer gets for it. If a gap fits no domain, the orchestrator carries it into the
closing rather than dropping it.


1. **`verdict`** is one of `strong`, `working`, `thin`, `not observed`. This is what the Quick Read and the
   final framing paragraph rank on. The framing paragraph the orchestrator drafts
   before dispatch is provisional and gets reconciled against these.
2. **`section`** is the prose for the full assessment. One to five short paragraphs, in
   Mo's voice, ready to drop under a `PRINCIPLE N:` heading. Italic bullets where the
   benchmark would use them.
3. **`quick_read`** is two or three sentences: one observation, one opportunity. This is
   what the two-page document uses. It is written fresh, not trimmed from `section`.
4. **`opportunities`** is the concrete moves named in the section, listed so the
   orchestrator can rank them and so Principle 7 can walk them in order.
5. **`gaps`** is what could not be assessed, with the questions the owner should answer.
   These go into the document, not into a private note.

Optionally, **`diagram`** proposes a full-page figure, for when this law carries the
biggest opportunity in the engagement. Only one or two diagrams per assessment, so a
chapter skill proposes and the orchestrator decides.

## What every chapter skill must contain

- The law in one sentence, and the book's image for it, so the writing can reach for the
  right metaphor and no other.
- The intake questions this law needs answered.
- The signals that the law is being obeyed, and the signals that it is being broken.
- The opportunity patterns this law generates, with what each one looks like at a
  one-person shop, a ten-person shop, and a growing company.
- How the AI angle enters for this law, always described by what it does and never by
  product name.
- Section-writing instructions: length, the four moves, what to open on.
- A worked example modeled on the benchmark.
- The vocabulary this law owns, and the vocabulary it must not borrow from other laws.

## Worked examples are not templates

Every chapter skill carries a worked example, and they are written well enough to be
copied. Do not copy them. If a sentence in your section has the same shape as a sentence in
the skill's illustration, you pattern-matched instead of writing, and the client will get a
document assembled out of someone else's cadence. Take the moves from the example. Leave
the sentences.

The tell: your section reads fine and none of it is quotable back to the intake record.

## Contested findings

Four findings get claimed by two laws each. The owner is fixed here so the same
recommendation does not reach the client twice under two names. The law that does not own
it may reference it in one clause and must not re-argue it.

| Finding | Owner | The other law's angle |
|---|---|---|
| Writing down how the work is done | Law 2, when the point is that the method can be handed off, run at another size, or sold in a deal | Law 3, when the point is that the output is the same every time and a second person can be held to it |
| Individual operators entering the market | Law 6 owns the market read: who is entering, what it means, who becomes an acquisition target | Law 4 owns it only as a channel decision, when the recommendation is to recruit them |
| Case studies and customer stories | Law 5 owns the finding: which customers are the success stories, and why | Law 4 owns publishing them into the garden |
| What a market pays a person for | Law 6 owns it as positioning against competitors who automate the person away | Law 3 owns it as the decision about what stays human inside your own operation |
| Somebody using AI tools without being asked to | Law 1 owns the fact that a decision has been open and nobody has looked | Law 2 owns what happens to the method once someone has looked |
| Nothing is written down | Law 2 owns it, and it is the only section that argues it | Laws 1, 3 and 5 each hit this and must reference it in one clause, not re-argue it. A reader told three times in three vocabularies stops reading |
| Where the money comes from, pricing and margins | Law 7, which asks what the customer gets for what they pay | Law 5 may note it when a lost bid was assumed to be about price |

## The vocabulary boundary

Each law has its own image. Mixing them is the most common way a draft reads as though
the framework was applied from outside instead of used.

| Law | Owns |
|-----|------|
| 1 | the mirror, the drill and the hole, intention before action, the conversation |
| 2 | the snowflake and the glacier, translation, the pattern, figurative scale |
| 3 | frequency, the note, the bell, the fog, entrainment, static, holding steady |
| 4 | active and passive, the fire and the garden, hunter and gardener, seed and soil |
| 5 | hot and cold, temperature, the coin, the autopsy, the continuous scale |
| 6 | rhythm, the swing, the kite, the wind, the tide, the arc, fit |
| 7 | the ledger, cause and effect, the free loaf, giving and extracting, the bay, the net |

**Every law's forbidden list is every other law's row.** Do not carry a short version of
this table into a chapter skill, because a short version is how an image goes missing from
a forbidden list and then turns up in a section. Name all six other laws' images or point
back here.

Two words need disambiguating. "Scale" in the ordinary sense (the size of a company, work
that scales) is plain English and every law may use it. Only the figures are owned: Law 2's
scale as in the snowflake against the glacier, and Law 5's scale as in one continuous
temperature. "Static" belongs to Law 3 and is not available to any other law as a synonym
for clutter.

A law may name another law by name when the finding hands off. The book does this and so should
the document. What it may not do is argue that finding again in its own imagery.
